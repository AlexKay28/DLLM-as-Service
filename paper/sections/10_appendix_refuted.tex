\section{Six Refuted Hypotheses}
\label{app:refuted} We set out to find regimes where the diffusion model beats the autoregressive
baseline, and generated hypotheses accordingly. Six were refuted. They are
reported here at the same length as the confirmations, because a paper whose
central methodological claim is ``price the baseline before you compare
against it'' has exactly one asset, and that asset is spent the moment a
negative result gets softened into a footnote. The distinction between these six and the eight-item ledger in
Appendix~\ref{app:defects} is worth stating plainly. There, our
\emph{harness was wrong}: the measurement itself was contaminated, so the
recorded number was incorrect. Here, our \emph{prediction was wrong}: the
measurement was sound, but the hypothesis it tested did not survive it. \begin{table}[h]

\end{table} \paragraph{Perturbation robustness, refuted in every category}
We predicted that denoising a whole canvas jointly would make the diffusion
model more tolerant of noisy input than left-to-right decoding. Across ten
perturbation categories at full intensity, it was worse in all ten. Two
categories --- whitespace and abbreviation --- left the autoregressive model
completely flat while costing the diffusion model $0.081$ and $0.005$ F1.
The largest ratio is separator changes, at $8.2\times$. One hypothesis for the
direction: left-to-right conditioning propagates input noise forward only,
while bidirectional conditioning also propagates it backward. We did not test
that, and it stays a hypothesis. \paragraph{Infilling, refuted for a reason worth stating}
The prediction was structural: with a fixed right context, suffix tokens are
never in the denoised set, so the diffusion model should preserve them
byte-identically by construction. The experiment ($S \in \{0,2,4,8\}$ suffix
fields, $n{=}50$) refuted it, but the mechanism matters --- the
autoregressive model scores $0.993$--$0.999$ because it trivially
regenerates the whole record from the prompt. This task is copy-heavy, and
copying is something autoregressive decoding does well. The structural
argument may still hold where the suffix is long and cannot be regenerated
from context, such as code infilling. It does not hold on short JSON fields. \paragraph{A pattern in the refutations}
Every refuted hypothesis had, at some earlier point, appeared to be
confirmed. In each case the apparent confirmation traced to an artifact of
our own harness rather than to a property of either architecture: the
bounded-deadline result was the completeness metric, the length-invariance
result was the contaminated metric, and the perturbation and refinement arms
ran a mis-provenanced model. The measurement discipline is not a preamble to
the results. It is what separates these six from the ones we kept.
