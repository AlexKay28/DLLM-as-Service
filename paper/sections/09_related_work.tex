\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Masked and discrete diffusion language models.} Discrete diffusion
was formalised for text by D3PM~\cite{austin2021d3pm} and sharpened by the
score-entropy~\cite{lou2024sedd} and simplified masked-diffusion
objectives~\cite{sahoo2024mdlm}, which established masked diffusion as the
practical variant. LLaDA~\cite{nie2025llada} and Dream~\cite{ye2025dream} are the
first openly released instruction-tuned models at a scale where serving
questions become meaningful, and are the models most of our diffusion
measurements use. Block diffusion~\cite{arriola2025blockdiffusion} interpolates
between autoregressive and diffusion decoding and motivates the
semi-autoregressive ablation of \cref{sec:neg-semiar}; our finding that block
splitting helps consistency only when the boundaries align with schema fields is
consistent with its framing of block structure as a modelling choice rather than
a decoding trick. DiffusionGemma~\cite{diffusiongemma} is the largest diffusion
model in our study. \todo{cite: confirm DiffusionGemma release venue, authors and
year; confirm Dream 7B citation form}

\paragraph{LLM serving systems.} Our AR baseline's cost profile is not a
property of the model alone but of the stack it runs on, and the stack is the
product of a decade of systems work. Continuous batching~\cite{yu2022orca} made
per-iteration scheduling practical; PagedAttention and
vLLM~\cite{kwon2023vllm} made the KV cache cheap enough that the resulting
batches actually fit; FlashAttention~\cite{dao2022flashattention} removed the
attention memory bottleneck underneath both. Together these are the reason the
AR batching multiplier we measure in \cref{sec:mechanism} is two orders of
magnitude, and the reason \cref{sec:method} argues that any comparison against a
naive generation loop is a comparison against a strawman. Serving support for
diffusion models is at a much earlier stage: the engine limitation described in
\cref{sec:two-walls} is a live upstream issue, not a settled design.
\todo{cite: upstream vLLM issue for the diffusion mixed-phase crash; dInfer}

\paragraph{Log parsing and anomaly detection.} Drain~\cite{he2017drain} is the
standard online template miner and the baseline of \cref{sec:neg-baselines};
LOGHUB~\cite{he2020loghub} provides the corpora, including the BGL split used
throughout this paper. Learned detectors --- DeepLog~\cite{du2017deeplog} and
LogBERT~\cite{guo2021logbert} --- operate on parsed template sequences rather
than raw lines, which is itself evidence for the decomposition we advocate:
template mining first, learned components on what the parser cannot produce.
Benchmarks of parser accuracy across corpora~\cite{zhu2019toolsandbenchmarks}
report the same structural-field ceiling we observe.

\paragraph{Baseline strength and reproducibility.} This is where our
methodological contribution sits, and the literature predicted our result.
Lipton and Steinhardt~\cite{lipton2019troubling} catalogue the failure mode
directly: improvements attributed to a new mechanism that are attributable to
tuning, to the baseline's handicap, or to the evaluation harness. Ferrari Dacrema
et al.~\cite{dacrema2019worrying} and Musgrave et al.~\cite{musgrave2020reality}
show what happens when the baselines in a subfield are re-run with equal care ---
in both cases the reported progress largely disappears. Our two AR-side defects
(\cref{sec:method}) are instances of exactly this: a decoding default that
penalised copy-from-input extraction, and a serving stack chosen without matching
the one the comparison assumed. Both errors ran in the same direction, flattering
the new architecture, which is the direction the reproducibility literature says
bias tends to run~\cite{sculley2018winners}. The practical prescription we draw
--- price the baseline before measuring the contender, and separate
architecture-internal claims from comparative ones --- follows
\cite{bouthillier2019unreproducible} in treating the measurement pipeline, not
the model, as the object under study. \todo{cite: an empirical-standards or
checklist reference for systems-measurement papers specifically}
