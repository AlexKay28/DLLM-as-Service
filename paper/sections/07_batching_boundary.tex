\section{The Batching Boundary}
\label{sec:batching}

The per-event crossover favours the diffusion model in a narrow region, but a
deployment serves an arrival stream, not one event. Under batching the picture
does not merely shift --- it inverts, by roughly two orders of magnitude
(Figure~\ref{fig:batching}).

\subsection{The gap, and a second runtime}

On vLLM the autoregressive model sustains \arBatchLo$\times$ at $L{=}128$ and
\arBatchHi$\times$ at $L{=}512$. DiffusionGemma on the same engine sustains
\dgBatchVllmHi$\times$ and \dgBatchVllmLo$\times$ --- but those numbers were
measured with the batch capped at 8 by the mixed-phase crash, so they are
lower bounds on what vLLM could do, not measurements of a ceiling.

The control that makes the result more than a bug report is LLaDA-8B on
dInfer: a different model family (dense rather than mixture-of-experts), a
different runtime, and a diffusion-native one. It sustains
\dgBatchMult$\times$, flat from batch 2 to batch 64. \emph{This measurement is
compromised:} dInfer's prefix and vicinity KV-cache paths error on prompts of
632 tokens or more, forcing a cache-less mode that inflates absolute latency
by roughly $30\times$ and swamps any block-length stepping effect. The
multiplier is not affected --- it is a ratio, and both numerator and
denominator are equally inflated --- but the claim that this is a
cache-enabled measurement is not defensible. Both of our diffusion serving
paths are therefore bounded by known engine defects: vLLM caps the batch at
8, and dInfer runs without caching. The true ceiling on an uncrippled stack
is unknown but lies at or above \dgBatchVllmHi$\times$, the highest lower
bound we measured.

\subsection{We tested the mechanism, and it is not the whole story}

The standing explanation for the ceiling is that bidirectional attention
invalidates the KV-cache reuse that makes autoregressive batching cheap,
leaving the diffusion model compute-bound at batch-1 with no bandwidth-bound
headroom for batching to convert. That explanation is plausible, and it is
testable in one direction: if KV-cache reuse is what creates the headroom,
removing it should remove the headroom.

It does not. Re-running the autoregressive batch sweep with prefix caching
disabled drops the multiplier from \cacheOnMult$\times$ to
\cacheOffMult$\times$ --- a real effect, and clearly the cache is doing work
--- but \cacheOffMult$\times$ is still more than twenty times the diffusion
ceiling of \dgBatchMult$\times$. Cache reuse therefore accounts for part of
the gap and cannot account for the rest.

\paragraph{How much of the mechanism we isolated}
The diffusion batching ceiling reproduces across two runtimes and two model
families, but \emph{both measurements are bounded by known engine defects}:
vLLM caps the batch at 8, and dInfer runs cache-less. The true ceiling on an
uncrippled stack is unknown but lies at or above \dgBatchVllmHi$\times$.
The KV-cache hypothesis is partly falsified as a complete explanation:
disabling prefix caching on the autoregressive arm drops its multiplier from
\cacheOnMult$\times$ to \cacheOffMult$\times$, still more than twenty times
the diffusion ceiling. Remaining candidates: mixture-of-experts routing that
varies across denoising steps; sheer model size, since the diffusion model
is \footprintRatio$\times$ larger; and kernel maturity. We disentangle none
of these.

\subsection{A free Pareto improvement at canvas $= 128$}

DiffusionGemma's canvas length is a configuration parameter, so we swept it.
At canvas $=128$ the base falls to \canvasOneTwentyEightBase\,ms
($-2.0\%$ relative to the canvas $=256$ measurement of 376.0\,ms;
\artifact{results/issue66\_canvas\_128.json},
\artifact{results/issue66\_canvas\_256.json}) \emph{and} 9-field F1 rises to
\canvasOneTwentyEightFone\ --- an improvement on both axes simultaneously.
At canvas $=64$ the base rises $48\%$, because the per-canvas fixed overhead
is now paid twice as often; that setting is not viable. This is the only
direct experimental evidence in the paper that block size, not denoising
schedule, is the interesting design variable, and it points the same way as
the structural argument in \S\ref{sec:laws}.

\subsection{The crossover does not move}

Neither canvas setting moves the per-event crossover. $L^{*}$ is set by the
prefill cost that dominates the base --- bounded above by 53--68\% at $K{=}8$
(\S\ref{sec:laws}) --- and prefill does not shrink when the canvas does.
Tuning the canvas trades quality against step count. It does not buy a
different crossover.
