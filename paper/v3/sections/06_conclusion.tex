\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Diffusion language models have a real latency advantage for structured
output: $1.8\times$ faster than the quality-comparable AR model even at natural
output length, inflated to $5.4\times$ at forced output $L{=}1024$. The
advantage comes from the cost shape --- a staircase that is flat inside each
block --- not from the diffusion process itself. The floor is mostly prefill,
and any decoder emitting fixed-size blocks would inherit the same shape.

The advantage does not survive deployment. A $1.8\times$ gap on a model
\footprintRatio$\times$ larger is not a deployment argument, and at matched
quality the AR model batches more than an order of magnitude better. What
survives is a narrow regime: long forced output, batch-1, schema validation,
and a data-centre footprint. Outside that regime, the line beats the
staircase.
