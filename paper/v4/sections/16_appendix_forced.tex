\section{Analytical Resolution of the Forced-Decoding Question}
\label{app:forced}

The forced comparison (Table~\ref{tab:niche}) charges AR-1.5B for
\texttt{min\_tokens} output that the parser discards. A reviewer asks:
does the advantage survive on a task whose \emph{natural} output length
is $\geq 512$ tokens? We answer this analytically, without running a new
experiment, by applying the fitted cost laws.

\paragraph{The cost laws are length-dependent, not forcing-dependent.}

AR-1.5B's latency is linear in output length, fitted from four measured
points ($L \in \{138, 256, 512, 1024\}$, $R^2 > 0.999$):
\begin{equation}
T_{\textsc{ar}}(L) = 142.3 + 3.79 \cdot L \quad\text{(ms)}
\end{equation}
DG's latency is the staircase:
\begin{equation}
T_{\textsc{dg}}(L) = 378.3 + 119.5 \cdot
\Bigl(\bigl\lceil \tfrac{L}{256} \bigr\rceil - 1\Bigr) \quad\text{(ms)}
\end{equation}
Neither law depends on \texttt{min\_tokens}; both depend only on
$L_{\mathrm{out}}$.

\paragraph{A naturally-long task exists.}

A multi-record extraction task --- emit a JSON array of $N$ structured
records from $N$ consecutive log lines --- produces output of
$N \times 140$ tokens naturally, without forcing. At $N{=}4$ the output
is $\sim$560 tokens; at $N{=}8$, $\sim$1120.

\paragraph{The advantage holds.}

Applying the fitted laws:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Task & $L_{\mathrm{out}}$ & AR-1.5B (ms) & DG (ms) & Ratio \\
\midrule
Single record (unforced) & 138 & 666 & 378 & $1.8\times$ \\
Multi-record $N{=}2$ & 280 & 1204 & 498 & $2.4\times$ \\
Multi-record $N{=}4$ & 560 & 2266 & 617 & $3.7\times$ \\
Multi-record $N{=}8$ & 1120 & 4390 & 856 & $5.1\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The $5.4\times$ advantage at $L{=}1024$ in the paper is within the range
predicted by the cost laws for any task producing that output length,
forced or natural. What the forcing artifact changes is not the ratio
but the \emph{interpretation}: at forced $L{=}1024$ the AR arm is charged
for discarded tokens; at natural $L{=}1024$ (multi-record $N{=}8$) it is
charged for tokens that are all used. The niche is real either way.

\paragraph{Caveat.}

This is an analytical projection, not a measurement. The cost laws are
fitted on single-record output; multi-record output may have different
prompt structure (longer input) and different token efficiency per
record. The projection assumes the per-token decode cost is unchanged,
which is the standard assumption for autoregressive decoding.
