\section{Does the advantage survive natural long output?}
\label{app:forced}

The comparison in Table~\ref{tab:niche} uses forced lengths for AR-30B
for \texttt{min\_tokens} output that the parser discards. A
multi-record extraction task --- emit a JSON array of $N$ structured
records from $N$ consecutive log lines --- produces output of
$N \times 140$ tokens naturally, without forcing. Applying the fitted
cost laws (Eqs.~\ref{eq:ar}--\ref{eq:dg}):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Task & $L_{\mathrm{out}}$ & AR-30B (ms) & DG (ms) & Ratio \\
\midrule
Single record (unforced) & 123 & 5900 & 379 & DG $15.6\times$ \\
Multi-record $N{=}2$ & 280 & 13435 & 498 & DG $27.0\times$ \\
Multi-record $N{=}4$ & 560 & 26791 & 618 & DG $43.4\times$ \\
Multi-record $N{=}8$ & 1120 & 53503 & 857 & DG $62.4\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The first row is measured; subsequent rows are cost-law projections.
The advantage grows because AR's linear cost grows far faster than DG's
staircase.
