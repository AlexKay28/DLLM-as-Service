\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}

All measurements are on one GPU; the fitted constants are A100-specific
but the shapes should transfer. The crossover is calibrated at a
reference prompt of $\sim$\refPrompt\,tokens and shifts with input
length.

\paragraph{The AR-30B arm is not yet verified.}

The AR-30B measurements differ from every other arm in three ways at
once, and all three plausibly slow the AR side. First, engine version:
every DG, AR-3B and AR-1.5B cell ran on vLLM 0.25.1 (torch 2.11.0,
CUDA 13.0), but the AR-30B ran on vLLM 0.11.0 (torch 2.8.0, CUDA
12.8). Second, CUDA graphs: the AR-30B harness sets
\texttt{enforce\_eager}, which the anchor harnesses do not, so the
AR-30B decoded without graph capture. Third, and most seriously, the
AR-30B harness read its inputs from the gold record file rather than
the raw log file, so the model was prompted with the answer it was
supposed to extract --- which is why its 9-field F1 is exactly
$1.0$ in every cell, and why its outputs are near-copies of the gold.

The consequence is that the AR-30B decodes at $48$\,ms/token against
the dense AR-3B's $6.3$\,ms/token --- a $7.6\times$ per-token penalty
for a model with the same active parameter count, which we do not
believe. We therefore report the AR-30B comparison as
\emph{unverified} rather than as a bound: the cost law
(Eq.~\ref{eq:ar}), Table~\ref{tab:niche}, the fifteenfold and
eightfold headline figures, the $77\times$ batching multiplier and the
cost-per-event table all inherit these three confounds and require
re-measurement on the pinned stack before they can be relied on. The
DG-side constants, the canvas step, the $K$ sweep and every AR-3B and
AR-1.5B result are unaffected, having been measured on the pinned
stack from raw log lines. This is the same class of defect as
correction~2 in Appendix~\ref{app:defects} --- a harness asymmetry
flattering the architecture we hoped would win --- found, as those
eight were, by re-reading committed artifacts.

The AR--DG quality difference is equivalent at a $\pm 0.015$ margin
($\Delta = \qualityGap$, 95\% CI $[-0.012, +0.011]$, $n = 300$). Note
that $\pm 0.015$ is looser than the $\pm 0.010$ margin we
pre-registered, at which the 95\% CI does not quite fit; equivalence
holds at the pre-registered margin only on the 90\% CI. This quality
comparison uses the dense AR-3B baseline, not the AR-30B MoE: the
9-field F1 of \arThreeBFnine\ was re-scored with the corrected parser
on the dense baseline, and the AR-30B was not re-scored at the same
parser setting. The latency comparison uses the AR-30B MoE cost law, so
the quality and latency arms are not the same model.

Several secondary results come from arms other than the MoE-matched
pair, and we flag them together rather than only at each use. The
per-event tail statistics (CV, max/median) in Table~\ref{tab:summary}
and Table~\ref{tab:contrast} are the dense AR-3B; the AR-30B was never
run per-event. The BGL$\to$HDFS generalisation was measured on the
dense AR-3B for the AR side. The perturbation, refinement and
bounded-deadline probes in Appendix~\ref{app:refuted} were run on
\LLaDA, a second diffusion model, against the dense AR-3B. And the
serial headline is a batch-1 result: the same AR-30B batches
$77\times$, so a deployment that can batch inverts the economics
entirely (\S\ref{sec:limits}).

The surviving niche has not been tested on a task whose natural output
length is $\geq 512$ tokens. The AR-30B cost law is fitted from a
five-point serial sweep with $R^2 = 0.9996$, but the model stops at
$\sim$123 tokens (EOS), so the slope is carried by the $L{=}32$ point
and the cluster at natural output. The advantage in serial mode is the
realized comparison; forcing AR to generate longer output would
increase its latency per the cost law, making DG's advantage larger.
Appendix~\ref{app:forced} works that projection through a multi-record
task whose natural output is long.

The niche has not been tested against grammar-constrained AR decoding,
which would eliminate over-generation, guarantee 100\% schema
validity, and remove the diffusion model's completeness advantage. An
attempt to run grammar-constrained decoding on the dense AR-1.5B
(vLLM 0.10.2, using the outlines~\cite{outlines2024} backend) produced
1.0\% schema-valid output --- the grammar enforced JSON structure but
the model padded string fields it could not answer, preventing the JSON
from closing. That is one model on one older engine, so it bounds
nothing; the comparison against a properly tuned grammar-constrained
AR remains untested.

The batching ceiling reproduces across two runtimes and two model
families --- DG on vLLM and \LLaDA\ on dInfer --- but both measurements
are engine-bounded; see
Appendix~\ref{app:batching}. A structural argument for why the ceiling
is likely architectural is in Appendix~\ref{app:batching_architectural}.

The cost-shape generalisation is inferred from one block-diffusion
model measured against one AR family. The shape claim is structural but
not tested on a second diffusion family.

The energy comparison uses three different idle baselines; the DG
energy was not re-measured at the AR-30B idle point, so the AR-30B vs
DG ratio is indicative rather than directly controlled.
