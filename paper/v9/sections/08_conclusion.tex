\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

DiffusionGemma offers a useful latency--quality tradeoff for the measured
serial log-extraction task. It returns substantially sooner than AR-30B,
and far more of its generations fit a tight service-time budget. The direct
quality comparison shows close extraction scores with a small DG deficit,
so the choice depends on whether that quality meets the task's needs.

This makes diffusion a candidate for time-constrained extraction and
dependent workflows where each response delays the next action. In such
workflows, generation-time savings can accumulate along the sequence of
actions. The operational test is the fraction of problems resolved correctly
before the deadline, including validation and retries; our serial timings
motivate that evaluation. Longer
required outputs may also favor its block-wise cost pattern, subject to
canvas boundaries and quality. AR's stronger batching gains favor serving
many independent requests economically. The useful comparison is acceptable
quality and on-time output at the intended concurrency, not a universal winner.
